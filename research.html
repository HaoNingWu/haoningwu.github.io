Global / Spectral × Sampling

Sampling theory asks when a finite set of nodes and positive weights captures a continuous spectral space without losing stability. Its central language links node geometry, discrete norm equivalence, quadrature, and discrepancy.

Keywords · Chebyshev–Lobatto points · node perturbations · scattered sampling · Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequalities · quadrature exactness · positive-weight quadrature · spherical t-designs · Sobolev discrepancy · QMC designs

Point sets and scattered sampling

Point perturbations and scattered configurations expose the geometric conditions behind stable discrete norms.

References
  1. H.-N. Wu, “When do perturbed Chebyshev–Lobatto points remain Chebyshev?” Preprint, arXiv:2606.23409 (2026).Gives a deterministic stability threshold for the Lebesgue constant under angular perturbations of Chebyshev–Lobatto nodes.
  2. H.-N. Wu, “Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequalities for scattered data on polygons.” Preprint, arXiv:2411.16584 (2024).Constructs quadrature rules for scattered points on polygons and studies the resulting MZ inequalities.

Quadrature and discrepancy

Exactness, positive weights, and discrepancy quantify how sampling rules transfer continuous information to computation.

References
  1. H.-N. Wu, “Hyperinterpolation beyond exact cubature: a spectral multiplier approach.” Preprint, arXiv:2605.17739 (2026).Uses Sobolev discrepancy to analyze discrete spectral multipliers without exact cubature or an MZ assumption.
  2. C. An and H.-N. Wu, “Spherical configurations and quadrature methods for integral equations of the second kind.” SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 64 (2026), 148–169.Here the emphasis is on spherical configurations, positive weights, and MZ-controlled quadrature as a stable sampling mechanism; its direct integral-equation role appears in (1,3).
  3. C. An and H.-N. Wu, “Bypassing the quadrature exactness assumption of hyperinterpolation on the sphere.” Journal of Complexity 80 (2024), 101789.Replaces exactness by an MZ property, allowing positive-weight rules on broader families of spherical point sets.
  4. C. An and H.-N. Wu, “On the quadrature exactness in hyperinterpolation.” BIT Numerical Mathematics 62 (2022), 1899–1919.Quantifies what happens when the usual degree-2n exactness requirement is reduced.

Global / Spectral × Approximation

Approximation theory studies how finite-dimensional models inherit the structure of the target and the data. In discrete settings, regularization and target singularities determine whether a nominally accurate scheme is also stable and efficient.

Keywords · polynomial approximation · hyperinterpolation · filtered approximation · spectral multipliers · regularization · thresholding · quadrature-based approximation · singular and oscillatory functions

Hyperinterpolation and its extensions

Hyperinterpolation and its extensions combine discrete polynomial approximation with regularization, thresholding, and adaptations to singular or oscillatory targets.

References
  1. H.-N. Wu, “Hyperinterpolation beyond exact cubature: a spectral multiplier approach.” Preprint, arXiv:2605.17739 (2026).Here the emphasis is the spectral multiplier as an approximation operator; its sampling and discrepancy role appears in (1,1).
  2. C. An, J. Ran, and H.-N. Wu, “The path of hyperinterpolation: a survey.” Dolomites Research Notes on Approximation 18.1 (2025), 135–145.Organizes the classical theory, relaxed exactness results, variants, and applications into one overview.
  3. C. An and H.-N. Wu, “Is hyperinterpolation efficient in the approximation of singular and oscillatory functions?” Journal of Approximation Theory 299 (2024), 106013.Shows why ordinary hyperinterpolation can be inefficient for singular or oscillatory targets and analyzes an efficient modification.
  4. C. An and H.-N. Wu, “Lasso hyperinterpolation over general regions.” SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 43 (2021), A3967–A3991.Applies soft thresholding to discrete polynomial coefficients to improve approximation in noisy-data regimes.
  5. C. An and H.-N. Wu, “Tikhonov regularization for polynomial approximation problems in Gauss quadrature points.” Inverse Problems 37 (2021), 015008.Introduces Tikhonov regularization into polynomial least squares and derives explicit formulas at Gauss points.

Global / Spectral × Computation

Numerical computation turns continuous equations into finite algebraic problems while preserving the geometry and stability of the underlying function space. The key questions differ between time-dependent PDEs and the discretization of integral operators.

Keywords · spectral Galerkin · quadrature-based Galerkin · product integration · Nyström methods · operator discretization · MZ stability · Sloan iteration · superconvergence

Direct discretization of operators

Differential and integral operators can be approximated directly by finite-dimensional spectral representations and quadrature formulas.

References
  1. C. An and H.-N. Wu, “Spherical configurations and quadrature methods for integral equations of the second kind.” SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 64 (2026), 148–169.Combines MZ-based hyperinterpolation with product integration for weakly singular second-kind integral equations on the sphere.
  2. H.-N. Wu and X. Yuan, “Breaking quadrature exactness: a spectral method for the Allen–Cahn equation on spheres.” Preprint, arXiv:2305.04820.Uses an MZ-type relation in place of exact quadrature to analyze a spherical spectral method for Allen–Cahn evolution.

Galerkin methods

Projection-based methods approximate an equation through finite-dimensional trial and test spaces rather than by directly discretizing the underlying operator.

References
  1. H.-N. Wu, “Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund stability of quadrature-based Galerkin schemes.” Applied Mathematics Letters 182 (2026), 110033.Identifies MZ stability as a direct criterion for the discrete inner product and the resulting Galerkin scheme.
